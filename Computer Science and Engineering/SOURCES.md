# Sources and reading policy

Source metadata and topic coverage were checked against author, publisher, university, project, or standards-body pages on 2026-10-26. This is a reading catalog, not redistributed textbook content. A publisher or companion page may require obtaining the book through a library or purchase; only texts explicitly offered by their authors or publishers are described as open.

Read only the selected themes named in each unit. Original checklist objectives and completion projects guide study and experimentation; they are not copied book exercises. The catalog does not claim the projects appear verbatim in the cited sources.

Official documentation evolves. Select a supported version, record it with your experiments, and follow the named concepts if a navigation heading moves. Book editions are fixed where their contents are being used. An older edition may still explain the selected foundations, but framework APIs should follow their official current documentation.

The `Primary book` label follows the repository format even when the source is an official guide, course text, or paper. Required second sources supply precisely named missing content. Extension books are optional. Sources illustrated in other programming languages are used for concepts; the primary project languages remain Python, TypeScript, and Rust.

The AI/data specialization follows the explicitly requested stack and the skills section of the supplied résumé. A keyword footer is not evidence of tool usage. Personal contact details and résumé text are not copied into this repository.

Some sites may intermittently block automated readers. Verification can use an official table of contents or university reading guide without implying unrestricted access to the full book. For example, the Cambridge front matter verifies the MacKay title and topics, while the author site is the reading destination.
