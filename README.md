# Structured reading atlases

This repository contains topic-by-topic learning paths from first principles through advanced study and research directions. These are **reading and study plans**, with focused completion tasks and legitimate source metadata.

| Subject | Study map | Complete topic index | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mathematics | [Start here](Mathematics/README.md) | [All units](Mathematics/TOPIC_INDEX.md) | Numeracy and proof through undergraduate, graduate, applied, and research-facing branches. |
| Computer science and engineering | [Start here](Computer%20Science%20and%20Engineering/README.md) | [All units](Computer%20Science%20and%20Engineering/TOPIC_INDEX.md) | Programming, theory, hardware, software engineering, systems, system design, security, data science, AI, and research projects. |
| Robotics | [Start here](Robotics/README.md) | [All units](Robotics/TOPIC_INDEX.md) | Mechanics, electronics, fabrication, actuation, sensing, embedded control, robot software, planning, manipulation, vision, reinforcement learning, and integration. |

Computer science is organized for deep knowledge, proofs, experiments, and substantial projects. Python, TypeScript, and Rust are its primary implementation languages. Application tracks use FastAPI, NestJS, Astro, React, and Axum, with HTML, CSS, and Tailwind. Its deeper data and AI branches follow the selected current stack; the broader CS foundations retain their full subject scope.

Robotics connects those foundations to physical machines. Its [workshop-assistant build path](Robotics/BUILD_PATH.md) develops a DUM-E-inspired arm and gripper through measurable stages: mechanical design, an instrumented joint, an integrated arm, calibrated vision, contact skills, task autonomy, and evaluated learning. Python, Rust, and TypeScript retain their roles in modeling and autonomy, firmware, and operator interfaces.

Each leaf folder contains `book_name.txt`. That file names a primary book or clearly identified official learning source, a precise topic or chapter theme to read, an ordered study checklist, and a concrete completion exercise. Read the indicated portion, solve problems or conduct the experiment, then move on. You do **not** have to read every recommended book cover to cover. Chapter numbers and pages vary by edition, so the stable selectors here are section titles and concepts. Use each subject's complete topic index to jump directly to a unit.

The order is a dependency guide rather than a rigid calendar. Some branches can be studied in parallel. Each subject map explains entry points, prerequisites, and continuations. Computer science links to the relevant Mathematics branches, and Robotics links to both Mathematics and CSE.

These are open-ended fields; no static repository can exhaust them. The atlases cover major foundational, undergraduate, graduate, applied, and research-facing branches at a useful level of detail. The [contribution rules](AGENTS.md) explain how to extend them without creating vague subject folders.

The subject atlases contain reading metadata and study guidance. The separate [reference PDF directory](pdfs/README.md) contains three books for which the repository owner confirmed public redistribution permission. Find other cited books through a library, publisher, or legitimate open edition. The [computer science source catalog](Computer%20Science%20and%20Engineering/SOURCES.md) and [robotics source catalog](Robotics/SOURCES.md) record official reading destinations and access types. For mathematics, the [American Institute of Mathematics approved open-textbook catalog](https://textbooks.aimath.org/textbooks/approved-textbooks/) and [OpenStax mathematics catalog](https://openstax.org/k12/math) are useful starting points.
